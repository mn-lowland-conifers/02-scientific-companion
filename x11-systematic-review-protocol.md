The study must provide spatial estimates for carbon found in aboveground biomass, as well as soils and peat; develop strategies that maximize mitigation of global climate change; and provide recommendations for maximizing climate resilience, encouraging biodiversity, and providing air- and water-quality benefits. A report with the results of the study must be submitted to the chairs and ranking minority members of the house of representatives and senate committees and divisions with jurisdiction over environment and natural resources by January 15, 2027. This is a onetime appropriation and is available until June 30, 2027. 

## 11.1 Questions & Framing

Within that mandate, this review aims to answer how harvested peatlands differ from non-harvested peatlands across characteristics that matter for climate mitigation and water quality - water table position and mercury transport (include GHG?). 

To frame the question about water quality in a way to measure outcomes, this meta-analysis will look at where the water table sits, and how much water leaves the site as streamflow or catchment outflow. This review stands on its own separate from the statewide carbon mapping work in Parts I and II of this document.

The population of this analysis is lowland conifer dominated peatlands and forested wetlands, with a primary focus on black spruce and tamarack stands and a secondary focus on northern white cedar and black ash swamps. Studies geographically come out of the north central United States and southern Canada, with studies taking place in Minnesota being the primary target.

The intervention is any event that removes the overstory, including but not limited to methods such as clearcutting, stripcutting, shelterwood cuts, partial cuts, precommercial thinning, group
selection, or girdling. Drainage only treatments are kept in the review but they are coded as a separate category from harvest.

The comparator is an uncut control, a pre-treatment period at the same site, or an older age class where a study used a chronosequence design instead of a before and after comparison.

## 11.2 Eligibility Criteria

Every study was sorted into one of four tiers, and that tier decides how the study gets used later on.

**Tier A** is the primary evidence. These are studies that match the population and intervention above and report at least one of the two outcomes. There are eighteen studies that fall into this tier with most of them coming from the Marcell Experimental Forest in Minnesota (Verry, Sebestyen) and the Quebec forested-wetland program by Plamondon, Roy, Prévost, Jutras, and Pothier.

**Tier B** includes six studies that are somewhat outside the main system. These include black ash wetland studies from Minnesota and Michigan and studies that are focused on a different question such as regime shifts or transpiration. These are used as supporting evidence but are kept separate from the main black spruce/tamarack results. 

Tier C is background context. It includes eight studies that are used for general information including topics such as water-balance and catchment studies from outside the main geographical area.
Tier D has three studies on rooting depth, transpiration response to water table, and literature reviews. Tier D is used to provide context and find additional references

## 11.3 Search Strategy

Searches ran across Web of Science Core Collection, Scopus, Agricola,
TreeSearch, and CAB Abstracts, and a separate pass through grey literature
sources that were likely to hold relevant work that was never published in a journal:
Minnesota DNR, the Minnesota Forest Resources Council, the Minnesota
Environment and Natural Resources Trust Fund, Canadian Forest Service COFRDA
and NODA reports, Ontario's Ministry of Natural Resources and Forestry, and
Quebec's Ministère des Ressources naturelles et des Forêts.

The search string combined a wetland-type block, a species block, a
harvest-terms block, and a hydrology-outcome block. This search criteria was slightly adapted for each database depending on the syntax.

> (peatland OR bog OR fen OR swamp OR "forested wetland" OR muskeg OR
> lowland) AND ("black spruce" OR *Picea mariana* OR tamarack OR *Larix
> laricina* OR "lowland conifer" OR "northern white cedar" OR "black ash")
> AND (harvest\* OR clearcut\* OR "clear-cut\*" OR logging OR thinning OR
> shelterwood OR silvicultur\*) AND ("water table" OR watertable OR
> groundwater OR streamflow OR runoff OR discharge OR "water yield" OR
> outflow OR hydrolog\*)
