# 7 Field Campaign Design

The goal of this project is to assess peat depth across Minnesota on state school
trust land. Historic peat depth data will be used alongside field data collected
during the summer of 2025 to develop a machine learning model to create a map of
peat depth across the state.

## 7.1 Equipment & Materials

## 7.2 Point Selection

Legacy data from the Minnesota Peat Inventory was visualized to ensure duplicate
sites were not sampled. New sampling sites were then selected by overlaying a
shapefile of land under state school trust ownership with a layer of soils mapped as
histosols (organic soils where peat is likely to exist). A new layer was created from
these overlapping areas, and 380 potential sampling locations were selected.

To further narrow down the selection, 97 prioritized points were subsetted from the
list of 380 based on their covertype attribute from the Minnesota DNR inventory. The
prioritized points are covered by black spruce lowlands, tamarack, and muskeg cover
types. These points also provide a good distribution of ages and densities (BA/acre),
including some nonforested areas.

## 7.3 Preparation

Sample bags will be pre-labeled before field work with a unique sample ID. The
sample ID will increase numerically starting at P001. The sample IDs will be
associated with sites on the field sampling sheet. Horizon depth and date of sampling
will be written on the bags in the field.

*Reference: `Peat Sampling Sheet.xlsx`*

## 7.4 Site and Transect Establishment

The onX Hunt smartphone app will be used as a GPS to navigate to selected sites. In
addition to being used as a GPS, the app also shows land ownership, which will be
used to verify that the crew remains on public land while traversing to points.

Throughout the field season, sites will use the naming convention:

```
2025MN(3-digit county fips code)xxx
```

For example, the second site in Ramsey County would be named `2025MN123002`.

At each site, a transect line will be established across the peatland with a
50–200 m distance between transect points.

The vegetation crew will randomly establish a plot center with a center pole. The
soil team will record 3 peat depth measurements at a 5 meter distance from the plot
center at 60°, 180°, and 300°. The depth measurement procedure can be found in
§7.6.

## 7.5 Full Description Procedure

*Reference: `ORGANIC DESCRIPTION FORM.xlsx`*

Along each transect line, 2–3 full description points of peat will be recorded. A
Russian peat auger will be used to extract a core.

After the point has been selected and marked, a recorder will write down site
information on the Organic Description Form, including site ID, GPS coordinates,
date, time, and sampling personnel.

Take a video recording of the site with narration of site characteristics and any
other information deemed necessary. A DJI Mini 3 drone will also be flown to capture
additional aerial photography of the site from a 20 m elevation.

### Core Extraction

Position the Russian peat auger at the designated sampling point and insert
vertically into the peat, minimizing disturbance to the sampling point as much as
possible. Drive the auger into the ground until the auger head is flush with the
surface. Once the auger is in position:

- Rotate the auger handle 180° to collect a half-cylinder peat sample
- Remove the auger vertically using auger pullers
- Once extracted, rotate the auger head back to the open position to access the
  collected peat sample

**If bagged samples will be collected from the core:**

- Cut the semicircular peat sample on the auger in half vertically
- Place in labeled sample bag with sample number and record the depth of the sample
- Record information in the next available row on the Peat Sampling Sheet
- Designate the sample type column on the peat sample sheet with "Db" for intact
  samples and "grab" for samples with a compromised volume

**For core description:**

- Keep remaining peat that was not sampled intact on the auger
- Transfer the peat carefully to the gutter using a putty knife, making sure to keep
  depth intact
- Arrange the peat vertically so the length of peat laid on the gutter corresponds
  to borehole depth
- Every few extractions, verify that peat length on the gutter matches the depth of
  the borehole using a tape measure

Continue extracting peat with the Russian auger until reaching the underlying
mineral soil. Collect samples as appropriate throughout the process. For peat depths
exceeding auger length, attach extensions using the hex pin to reach greater depths.
The completed gutter will display vertically arranged peat cores representing the
complete soil profile.

### Description

- Photograph the complete profile at 40 cm increments. Attempt to keep the entirety
  of the profile in shade for the picture if possible.
- Record an additional video with slow, continuous movement down the entire core
  length.

Complete a detailed profile description on the Organic Description Form documenting
the following characteristics:

- Top and bottom depths for each horizon
- Horizon designation
- Munsell color
- Von Post humification score (H1–H10)
- Fiber content percentage
- Texture analysis and clay/sand percentages (when applicable for mineral
  components)
- Fragments, redox features, ped void characteristics
- Whether the horizon was sampled

*Reference: `Peat Sampling Sheet.xlsx`*

## 7.6 Depth Measurement Procedure

In addition to full description points, depth measurements will also be taken
across each transect using a tile probe. Insert the tile probe at each point and
drive it down with a slide hammer attachment. Add extensions to the probe until the
mineral soil has been reached. Once the mineral soil is hit, wrap a cable tie around
the shaft of the probe at the surface and extract the probe. Measure the length of
the tile probe from the tip of the auger head to the cable tie and record this as
peat depth at that location.

## 7.7 Samples

After samples are taken, ensure that all bags are properly sealed and labeled. After
fieldwork is completed each day, weigh samples and cross-check them on the Peat
Sampling Sheet to ensure that all information is accurate. Open bags to start drying
overnight until transportation back to campus at the end of the week.

## 7.8 Shifting Sites & Additional Selection

During the field season there were occasions when additional sites needed to be
selected. Causes for additional sites were accessibility issues due to road
construction and completing the predetermined sites for a given week. To select new
sites, histosols within a drivable area from lodging locations were identified using
SoilWeb. Public land ownership was then verified using the onX Hunt app.

### General sampling design

Plots will be established along an established transect running through the peatland in the direction that allows for the longest straight line running through the stand. The number of plots per peatland will depend on the transect length, but sites should have a minimum of five and a maximum of eight plots, see Table 1. Plot centers should be at least 50m apart.

**Table 1.** Number of sampled plots relative to total transect length.

| Transect length | Number of plots |
|---|---|
| 200-299 m | 5 |
| 300-399 m | 6 |
| 400-499 m | 7 |
| 500+ m | 8 |

For each plot, vegetation for the overstory, understory, and herbaceous plants will be assessed as shown in Fig. 1.

**Data sheet information:** At the top of the data sheet, record site number, plot number, date (MM/DD/YYYY) and circle the names of crew members collecting data. Add first initial and last name of any additional crew members not listed on the data sheet.

**Native plant community class:** Attempt to determine the NPC class using *Field Guide to the Native Plant Communities on Minnesota*, starting on pg. 9. You do not need to determine the NPC down to type or subtype (ex. FPn63, not FPn63a)/

**Photos:** Photos should be taken at each plot, one photo in each cardinal direction. Try to get an idea of the foreground and background in each photo. You may take additional photos to represent understory and overstory. Include plot information in each photo. Upload all photos to the Google Drive at the end of each day.

**Plot center coordinates:** Place the GPS at plot center. Mark a new point and average the location for at least 2 minutes or until the certainty on the GPS screen reaches 100%. Save the point with the word "NEW" at the end. Record the coordinates in decimal degrees on the data sheet.

**Densiometer:** Take 4 densitometer readings at breast height from plot center, one facing in each cardinal direction. Record the number of "dots" that represent canopy openings (out of 96 total). These numbers will later be multiplied by 1.04 to obtain the percent of overhead area not occupied by canopy. See instructions inside the cover of the densiometer.

### Overstory trees and saplings

**Overstory trees (≥10 cm dbh) and saplings (≥2.5 cm to <10 cm dbh):** For all tree species, starting from 0 degrees record species, dbh (cm), deterioration category (1-8, see Figure 2 and Table 1) percent live crown ratio (canopy cover in increments of 10%) (see Fig 3). If trees are infected with eastern spruce dwarf mistletoe (EDM), record "1" for present or "0" for absent. Measure the height of every 10th tree, but if the 10th tree is a dead tree, measure the height of the next living tree instead. Additionally, measure the height of the first dead tree.

For shrub species in this size class, tally individuals greater than 2.5 cm by genus and live/dead status. You do not need to record dbh, LCR, EDM, or deterioration category for shrubs. See Appendix A for which species are considered trees vs shrubs.

### Seedlings

**Large seedlings (height ≥1 m, dbh <2.5 cm, trees that have escaped browse):** Tally all large seedling-sized tree species by species in each orange plot (r=2.42 m). Orange circle subplots are located 5 m from the plot center at 0 degrees, 120 degrees, and 240 degrees. For species with multiple stems, count each stem coming out of the ground as one individual. If the stem forks below the ground, it should be counted as two individuals. Count stump sprouts as one individual. Do not count stems of shrubs in this category (see Appendix A species list for shrubs vs trees). The percent cover of these species will be estimated instead.

**Small seedlings and emergent seedlings (<1 m tall):** Tally all small seedling and emergent-sized merchantable tree species by species in two size classes (small seedlings: 30 cm ≤ height < 1 m and emergent: < 30 cm) in each of the three green circle subplots (r=1.49 m). Green circle subplots are located 5 m from the plot center at 0 degrees, 120 degrees, and 140 degrees. Do not count stems of shrubs in this category (see Appendix A species list for shrubs vs trees). The percent cover of these species will be estimated instead.

### Herbaceous plants

At each subplot, place a 1m² frame so that, with your back to plot center, the closest left corner of the frame is at the center of the subplots and the left and right edges of the frame are angled in the same direction as the subplot (Fig. 1). Within this frame, estimate the percent cover of each herbaceous species using the Braun-Blanquet cover classes below (Table 3) by functional class. Functional classes include tall shrubs (see Appendix A for definition), small shrubs (see Appendix A), graminoids (grasses, sedges, and rushes), forbs, ferns and fern allies (including Licopodium spp), Sphagnum spp., other mosses (not Sphagnum spp.), down dead wood (coarse and fine woody debris), standing water/muck, leaf/needle litter (not including dead grasses), and cattails (Typha spp.) Percent cover for the quadrant can total over 100%.

**Table 3.** Braun-Blanquet cover classes.

| Class | Percent cover |
|---|---|
| T | < 1%, trace |
| 1 | 1-5% |
| 2 | 6-25% |
| 3 | 26-50% |
| 4 | 51-75% |
| 5 | >76% |

### CWD transects

A 10m long CWD transect begins at each subplot center, running in the direction of the subplot (see Fig 1). Using a measuring tape, walk the transect and tally CWD only if its central longitudinal axis intersects the transect (Figure 4). CWD must be ≥ 10 cm in diameter at the point of intersection and ≥ 1 m long to be tallied. For each tallied CWD piece, record diameter at intersection (to the nearest 0.5 cm, measured with a large caliper) and decay class (see Table 4).

CWD includes downed, dead tree and shrub boles, large limbs, and other woody pieces that are severed from their original source of growth and on the ground. CWD also includes dead trees (either self-supported by roots, severed from roots, or uprooted) that are leaning > 45 degrees from vertical.

CWD does not include: dead trees leaning 0 to 45 degrees from vertical, dead shrubs self-supported by their roots., trees showing any sign of life, stumps that are rooted in the ground (i.e., not uprooted), dead foliage, bark or other non-woody pieces that are not an integral part of a bole or limb (bark attached to a portion of a piece is an integral part), or roots or main bole below the root collar.

**Tree cores:** Tree cores should be taken at breast height from the first 2-3 living height trees greater than 5 cm dbh. Up to 3 cores can be taken per plot. If a plot has less than three living height trees >5cm dbh, take 2-3 cores from the first living trees on the plot >5cm dbh. We want a total of at least 4 cores per transect, taken from at least two different plots. If a plot has no trees >5 cm dbh, you can skip taking cores on that plot, as long as you end up with at least 4 total cores on a transect.
